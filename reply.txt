13. Josh – on “Reporting”
Q: Curious about the goal of this? i.e. is it due to an external commitment? Or to keep ourselves accountable?

Mainly to keep ourselves accountable: are we meeting the SLA, which teams are overloaded, and where severity or SLAs need recalibrating. There’s no external commitment today, but the same reports would cover a customer questionnaire or audit. The weekly view will be an automated Jira dashboard, so there’s no manual reporting effort.


Agreed on using the incident process, but only for real emergencies. A CVE on its own is not an incident, and it shouldn’t be treated as urgent or handed to the incident/on-call team just because a scanner rates it Critical.

A CVE goes to the incident process only when all three of these are confirmed:
```
It is being exploited. There’s confirmed active exploitation in the wild (for example, it’s listed in CISA’s Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue), and a working exploit is publicly available.
It can be reached by people who shouldn’t have access. The affected system is reachable by unauthorised users; for example, it’s internet-facing or accessible without authentication.
Security has verified it. The vulnerable component is actually present and in use on that system, not just flagged by the scanner.

If any one of these isn’t met, it follows the normal SLA process: a Jira ticket in the owning team’s backlog (Critical 7 days, High 30 days), with no paging and no incident.

If all three are met, Security raises it through the incident process so on-call can act, and we check for signs of compromise alongside the fix.
```
